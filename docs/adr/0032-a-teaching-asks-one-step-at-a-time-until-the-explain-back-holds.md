# A teaching asks, one step at a time, until the user's explain-back holds

**Amends ADR 0020** on the shape of the lesson. ADR 0020 had the teacher reply once, at most a screen, and never ask a question back. The user found that a lesson they read is not a decision they understand, and asked for a small question to gauge them first, then teaching from there, still Socratic (spec 11 of `docs/design/deferred-ledger/architecture.md`). We decided that the teacher is still the existing `pilot-teacher`, still on Sonnet, with the same tools, and teaches by six rules, each citing its source in `agents/pilot-teacher.md` and `skills/teaching-a-decision/SKILL.md` (L14): the first reply is one diagnostic question, usually yes/no (Ausubel 1968, secondary); one step per reply, never a sub-step drip (VanLehn 2011); ask rather than tell, and never answer its own question in the same reply (Roediger & Karpicke 2006); once the steps are in, an explain-back, re-explaining only the missed part (AHRQ teach-back; Chi et al. 1994); "you have it" is a criterion, not a turn count: the explain-back states what is decided, the main alternative and why the default (Bloom 1968); and on "just tell me" or two stalls, a short worked step (Nye, Graesser & Hu 2014; the Khanmigo abandonment, Hechinger Report 2026). The loop rides on the resume the pilot already does for a follow-up. Each teacher report ends `teaching: live` or `teaching: done`, and the pilot's rule is that while a teaching is live a typed reply on the thread resumes the teacher, an option press is the decision whenever it comes, and after `done` a typed reply is the decision again (L15). Rejected: a new tutor agent, or the explorer teaching (the teacher already is a separate agent, and the explorer stays the reader); questions with no fallback (students abandoned Khanmigo when it only asked); a cap on turns (mastery is a criterion, not a count); a deck-side teaching state, or a teacher that reads or waits on the deck (ADR 0020's reason stands: a second waiter swallows other threads' answers); and asking the user whether a typed reply was an attempt or an answer (a question about a question).

**Consequences**: a teaching now costs one Sonnet resume per user attempt rather than one spawn in all; the teacher still never resolves, holds or reads, so ADR 0020's tool list and its reasons are unchanged; the only new machinery is the report's last line, and the pilot has to read it to route the next typed reply; an option press ends a teaching at any point without the teacher hearing of it; and the loop is verified against a live deck only after release, since an agent definition cannot be exercised from the session that edits it.
